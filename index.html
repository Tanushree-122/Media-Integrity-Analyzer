🔍 Before we begin

Learn to spot manipulation — by catching it yourself.

This is a hands-on lesson, not a quiz. You'll read two short pieces of fictional media, react honestly, guess what's off before you're told, and then see a full breakdown of the techniques used.

Everything you see — headlines, posts, "studies," and quotes — is invented for this exercise. The techniques, however, are real and appear constantly in news, ads, and social feeds.

  • Challenge 1 — Headline Detective: compare a headline's claim to what the article actually says.

Concept

The Headline Gap

Headlines are written to make you click — not necessarily to tell you the truth.

Publishers and creators earn attention (and often money) from clicks, so headlines are frequently optimized for emotional punch rather than accuracy. This creates a gap between what a headline implies and what the article actually says.

Why it matters: Most people never read past the headline. If it exaggerates or misrepresents the story, that distorted version is the one that spreads — through shares, screenshots, and group chats.

In everyday life: You'll see this on news apps, video thumbnails, and shared links. Comparing a headline's claim to the article's actual evidence is one of the single most useful media literacy habits you can build.

Concept

Emotional Triggers

Content that makes you feel something strongly is far more likely to get shared.

Creators and recommendation algorithms both reward strong reactions, so a lot of what you scroll past has been deliberately engineered to trigger fear, outrage, or excitement before you've had a chance to think it through.

Why it matters: Strong emotion narrows your thinking. When you're angry or scared, you're less likely to check facts and more likely to share immediately — which is exactly the behavior manipulative content is designed to produce.

In everyday life: Watch for urgency ("before it's too late"), moral outrage, fear language, and appeals to identity. These patterns show up constantly in ads, political content, and viral posts.
